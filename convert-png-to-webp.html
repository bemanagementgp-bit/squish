    Convert PNG to WebP: how it works

    WebP was designed to replace both JPEG and PNG on the web. It supports an alpha channel like PNG, but encodes with modern prediction and entropy coding, so the same logo or illustration ends up a fraction of the size.

    Squish converts PNG to WebP locally in the browser. It is free, needs no sign-up, handles up to 10 files per batch, and never uploads your artwork to a server.

    What the conversion does to your file

    Select WebP in the format menu and set a quality. Here is what to expect:

    • Transparency is preserved. Soft shadows and anti-aliased edges survive; there is no white box around your logo.
    • Size drops 60–80% for typical graphics at quality 80. A 480 KB PNG icon set often becomes 70–110 KB.
    • Quality 85–95 is the safe range for flat UI graphics with sharp text; photos with transparency can go lower, around 75.
    • Metadata is removed. The canvas encoder writes a clean file without EXIF or embedded profiles.

    Compare the before and after preview in Squish at 100% zoom before downloading if the asset has fine lines.

    Browser support and fallbacks

    WebP is rendered by Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Opera, Android browsers and Safari 14 or newer (macOS Big Sur and iOS 14, released in 2020). Global support sits above 97% of users, so for most sites you can switch outright.

    If you must serve users on older Safari or embedded webviews, keep the PNG and use a <picture> element: list the WebP in a <source type="image/webp"> and the PNG in the fallback <img>. Email clients are a different story; Outlook on Windows still does not display WebP, so keep PNG or JPEG for newsletters.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does WebP support transparency like PNG?

    Yes. WebP has a full 8-bit alpha channel, in both lossy and lossless modes, so converted logos and icons keep their transparent backgrounds.

    Is WebP lossy or lossless?

    Both exist. Browser-based encoders, including the one Squish uses, write lossy WebP with a quality setting, which is what gives the large size reduction. At quality 90 or above the result is visually indistinguishable from the PNG.

    Can I convert many PNGs at once?

    Yes. Drop a folder in, choose WebP once and all files are converted together. The free plan processes 10 images per batch.

    Will my images be uploaded during conversion?

    No. The conversion runs in your browser; your files never touch a server.
