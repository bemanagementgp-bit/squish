    Compress Images for Your Website: how it works

    Oversized images are the most common reason a page fails the Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) threshold of 2.5 seconds. A 4 MB photo straight out of a phone camera can be served at 150 KB with no visible difference on screen.

    Squish compresses, resizes and converts images entirely inside your browser. It is free, there is nothing to install, and your files never leave your computer. Drop up to 10 images at a time, pick a format and quality, and download the results.

    Target sizes that keep Core Web Vitals green

    Use these numbers as a starting point when preparing images for a website:

    • Hero or banner images: 1920 px wide, under 200 KB. Choose 2560 px only if the image fills the viewport on retina screens.
    • Content and blog images: 1200–1600 px wide, 60–120 KB.
    • Thumbnails and cards: 400–800 px wide, under 40 KB.
    • Logos and icons: PNG or WebP with transparency, usually under 20 KB.

    In Squish, set Max dimension to the width your layout actually renders and move the quality slider to 70–80. For JPEG and WebP, that range typically cuts file size by 60–85% while artefacts remain invisible at normal zoom.

    Pick the format per image, not per site

    WebP is the best default for web delivery: it is roughly 25–35% smaller than JPEG at equal visual quality and it supports transparency, so it replaces both JPEG and PNG in most cases. Every current browser renders it, including Safari since version 14.

    Keep JPEG when the image will also be reused in email or older CMS plugins that cannot read WebP. Keep PNG only for flat graphics where every pixel must be exact, such as screenshots of UI with text. Tick Keep smaller so Squish returns the original whenever its re-encode would not actually save bytes.

    Workflow for a full site

    1. Export images from your design tool or camera at the largest size you need.
    2. Open Squish, drag a folder of images, set the format to WebP and max dimension to 1920 px.
    3. Download the results and upload them to your CMS or /assets folder.
    4. Run PageSpeed Insights again and compare the LCP figure and the "Properly size images" audit.

    Because the encoder runs on your machine, batches of large photos finish in seconds and nothing is stored anywhere afterwards.

    Frequently asked questions

    What image size is best for a website?

    Match the pixel width to the largest size the image is displayed at, usually 1920 px for full-width banners and 1200–1600 px for article images, then compress to under 200 KB for hero images and under 100 KB for everything else.

    Does compressing images improve SEO?

    Indirectly, yes. Page speed and Core Web Vitals are ranking signals, and image weight is usually the biggest factor in LCP. Smaller images also lower bounce rates on mobile connections.

    Will Squish upload my images anywhere?

    No. Compression uses the browser's own canvas encoder, so files stay on your device. You can even use the tool offline once the page has loaded.

    Can I compress many images at once?

    Yes. Drag a whole folder in. The free plan handles batches of 10 images up to 10 MB each; Pro removes the batch limit and adds ZIP download.
