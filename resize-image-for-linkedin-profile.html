    Resize an Image for Your LinkedIn Profile Photo: how it works

    LinkedIn rejects profile photos over 8 MB and quietly downsamples anything larger than it displays, which can leave a professional headshot looking soft. Preparing the file yourself at the right dimensions avoids both problems.

    Squish resizes and compresses the photo in your browser for free. There is no account, no watermark, and the image is never uploaded to a server, which is reassuring for a picture of your own face.

    LinkedIn's profile photo specs

    • Shape: square source; LinkedIn displays it as a circle, so keep the face centered with headroom.
    • Recommended size: 400 × 400 px minimum; 800 × 800 px gives a crisper result on retina screens.
    • Maximum: 8 MB and 7680 × 4320 px.
    • Formats: JPG, PNG or GIF.

    Crop to a square in your phone or desktop editor first. Then in Squish set Max dimension to 640 px or 1280 px (either is fine; the larger option is still tiny), pick JPEG and quality 85. The result is usually 60–150 KB and looks identical to the original in the circular frame.

    Other LinkedIn image sizes

    While you are at it, the same workflow covers the rest of your profile:

    • Background banner: 1584 × 396 px, under 8 MB. Resize to 1600 px max in Squish and compress to 150–300 KB.
    • Company page logo: 300 × 300 px, PNG with transparency if the logo needs it.
    • Post images: 1200 × 627 px for link previews, 1080 × 1080 px for square posts.

    Export banners and post images as JPEG at quality 75–80. Logos stay PNG. All of these can be processed together in one batch, and because Squish strips EXIF, no camera or GPS metadata travels with your photo to a public profile.

    Frequently asked questions

    What size should a LinkedIn profile picture be?

    A square between 400 × 400 and 800 × 800 px, under 8 MB. Around 100 KB as a JPEG is plenty.

    Why does my LinkedIn photo look blurry?

    Either the source was smaller than 400 px and got upscaled, or it was huge and LinkedIn downscaled it harshly. Resizing to 800 px at quality 85 before uploading fixes both.

    Can I use a PNG for my profile photo?

    Yes, LinkedIn accepts PNG. For a photograph, JPEG is much smaller with no visible difference; PNG only makes sense for logos or graphics.

    Is my photo stored by the tool?

    No. Squish processes the image locally in your browser and nothing is sent to or kept on a server.
