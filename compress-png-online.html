    Compress PNG Online: how it works

    PNG is lossless, which is why a 1440p screenshot can weigh 2–5 MB while an equivalent JPEG would be 300 KB. The format stores every pixel exactly, so simple quality sliders have no effect on it.

    Squish gives you three honest ways to shrink a PNG in the browser, for free and with no upload: a lossless re-encode that strips metadata, a resize to the dimensions you actually need, and a conversion to WebP that keeps transparency.

    Three ways to get a smaller PNG

    • Lossless re-encode. Keep the format as PNG and Squish rewrites the file through the browser's encoder, dropping EXIF, color profiles and text chunks. Savings are modest on already-optimized files, which is why the Keep smaller option returns the original when nothing was gained.
    • Resize. This is where PNG shrinks the most. A 2880 px retina screenshot resized to 1440 px holds a quarter of the pixels and usually a quarter of the bytes. Set Max dimension to the display width you need.
    • Convert to WebP. Lossy WebP with an alpha channel is commonly 60–80% smaller than the PNG original and still supports transparency, so logos, UI mockups and stickers stay clean.

    When PNG is still the right choice

    Stick with PNG when exact pixels matter: screenshots that will be zoomed, pixel art, QR codes, line art with hard edges and anything that will be edited again later. Text in a lossy format grows faint ringing around the letters at low quality, which never happens in PNG.

    For photos, PNG is the wrong container. A photograph saved as PNG is 5–10× larger than the same image as JPEG at quality 80 with no perceptible benefit. In that case choose JPEG in Squish's format menu; transparency is replaced with white, which the tool notes before you convert.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why doesn't the quality slider change my PNG size?

    PNG is lossless, so there is no quality parameter to lower. To reduce a PNG, resize it or convert it to WebP or JPEG. Squish explains this in the format hint when PNG output is selected.

    Can I compress a PNG and keep transparency?

    Yes. Both PNG output and WebP output preserve the alpha channel. Only JPEG flattens transparent areas to white.

    Is there a file size limit?

    Free use covers PNGs up to 10 MB each and 10 files per batch. Pro raises the per-file limit to 50 MB.

    Are my screenshots uploaded?

    No. Everything runs in the browser, which matters for screenshots that may contain private data or unreleased work.
